E/M code selection: medical decision making (MDM) or total time

Office/outpatient, hospital inpatient/observation, consultation, and home visits are leveled by MDM or by total physician/QHP time on the date of the encounter, whichever is more favorable and documented. History and examination are performed as medically appropriate but do not determine the level. ED visits (99281–99285) are leveled by MDM only.

New versus established patient

A patient is established if any professional service was received from the physician/QHP, or another physician/QHP of the exact same specialty and subspecialty in the same group, within the past three years. Coverage by another clinician is classified as it would have been by the unavailable clinician. APNs and PAs are treated as the same specialty as their supervising physician. MFM and general OB/GYN may be treated as different subspecialties by CPT, but Medicare uses specialty code 16 for both; confirm payer policy.

Same-day services

  • Modifier 25: a significant, separately identifiable E/M on the same day as a procedure or preventive visit. The E/M documentation must support work beyond the procedure's usual pre-service evaluation and beyond the preventive service.
  • Preventive and problem visit on the same day: report the preventive code plus the problem-oriented E/M with modifier 25, leveled only on the problem-oriented work.
  • Minor procedures (0- and 10-day global): the decision to perform the procedure is included; an E/M is reported only when separately identifiable (modifier 25).
  • Major procedures (90-day global): the decision-for-surgery E/M the day before or day of surgery is reported with modifier 57.

Telehealth

Payer policy determines code selection. Options include office/outpatient E/M 99202–99215 with place of service 02/10 and modifier 95 (or 93 for audio-only where allowed), or CPT telemedicine codes 98000–98015. Verify each payer's current policy before reporting.

Documentation essentials for any E/M

  • Reason for the encounter and each problem addressed, with status (e.g., stable, worsening, new with uncertain prognosis).
  • Data: which external notes were reviewed (source), which results were reviewed, which tests were ordered, independent interpretation (what and the finding), and discussion with external clinicians (who and the content).
  • Management: medications started/changed/continued, procedures considered or scheduled, admission or escalation decisions, and risk considerations.
  • Total time if time is used to select the level.
  • A prenatal flowsheet alone does not support an E/M level.

CPT 2027 maternity care: phase-based reporting (effective January 1, 2027)

Global maternity packages are deleted. Care is reported in four phases: antepartum (E/M per encounter), labor management (per calendar date), delivery, and postpartum (E/M per encounter). CMS adopted the AMA CPT maternity codes in the CY 2027 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule final rule; the proposed maternity G-codes were not finalized.

Straightforward requires all: singleton vertex; routine maternal/fetal monitoring; fetal monitoring not requiring physician/QHP intervention; normal progression or routine induction/augmentation; stable medical conditions requiring no additional management during labor; no previous cesarean. Complex is any labor management not meeting all straightforward criteria (e.g., multiple gestation, FHR abnormality requiring intervention, prolonged first or second stage, previous cesarean, maternal condition requiring added management). If complexity escalates during a calendar date, report complex for that date.

Initial day applies when any is met: first calendar date labor management is needed or induction begins; the physician/group has not previously provided labor management during this admission; transfer to a new facility after labor management elsewhere; a different specialty/subspecialty assumes care for reasons other than coverage. Otherwise report subsequent day, once per calendar date at the highest level, including the delivery date when labor management is performed.

Hospital inpatient/observation E/M (99221–99223, 99231–99236) is not reported on the same date as 59080–59083 by the same physician/group. An E/M at another site before admission (office, ED) may be reported with modifier 25. A scheduled cesarean without labor has no labor management code. An unplanned cesarean during labor: labor management may be reported (59081/59083). Multiple gestation: one labor management code per date (complex).

Antepartum and postpartum

Each encounter is leveled separately by MDM or total time. A prenatal flowsheet alone does not support an E/M level. Per AMA guidance citing CPT Assistant, a normal pregnancy may be counted as a problem addressed in the moderate category (chronic illness with exacerbation, progression, or side effects of treatment); data and risk must still be met for the overall level. Modifier TH (obstetrical treatment/services, prenatal or postpartum) identifies maternity services; confirm payer policy. Routine postpartum care on the delivery date is included in the delivery code; discharge codes 99238/99239 are not reported on the delivery date. Postpartum hospital days after the delivery date use subsequent hospital care, discharge, or critical care codes.

Pregnancies spanning 2026–2027

  • 2026 antepartum visits follow 2026 rules: 1–3 visits, E/M per visit; 4–6 visits, 59425; 7 or more, 59426. Non-routine encounters are reported separately.
  • Antepartum visits on or after January 1, 2027: E/M per encounter.
  • Delivery on or after January 1, 2027: 2027 codes. Continuous labor management spanning December 31–January 1 is reported once, with the initial code on January 1, 2027.
  • Delivery in 2026 (including December 31): report the 2026 code by delivery method; postpartum services on or after January 1, 2027 may be reported with E/M (with TH).
  • Confirm transition policy with each payer.

Teaching physician rules (Medicare; many commercial payers follow similar policies)

  • E/M: the teaching physician must be present for the key or critical portions of the service and personally document participation in the management of the patient. A resident note alone does not support billing.
  • Time-based E/M: only time the teaching physician personally spent, including time present with the resident, counts toward the level.
  • Surgery: the teaching physician must be present for the key or critical portions and immediately available throughout the procedure; overlapping-surgery documentation requirements apply.
  • Minor procedures (under 5 minutes): the teaching physician must be present for the entire procedure.
  • Deliveries: the teaching physician must be present for the delivery to bill the delivery service.
  • Primary care exception (GE): applies only at qualifying sites and to a limited set of lower-level E/M services; verify site eligibility with compliance before use.
  • Assistant at surgery: Medicare generally does not pay a physician assistant-at-surgery in a teaching hospital when a qualified resident is available; modifier 82 requires documentation that no qualified resident was available.

Source: CMS Medicare Claims Processing Manual, Chapter 12, Section 100 (Teaching Physician Services); CMS MLN “Teaching Physicians, Interns & Residents Guidelines”; CPT Appendix A (Modifiers). Confirm Holy Name compliance policy for resident billing.
